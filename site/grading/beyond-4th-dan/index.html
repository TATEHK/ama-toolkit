History of Taekwondo

Taekwondo is a Korean martial art. The name means "the way of the foot and fist": Tae means foot or kick, Kwon means fist, and Do means way. It grew out of traditional Korean fighting arts, was shaped during the 20th century, and is now an Olympic sport practised around the world.

Roots

Taekkyeon is a traditional Korean kicking art and a forerunner of Taekwondo. It moves in a flowing, rhythmic way and has been passed down for centuries. Taekkyeon is now on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list.

During the Japanese occupation of Korea (1910 to 1945), Korean culture was suppressed, including its martial arts. Many Koreans trained in Japanese karate during this time. After 1945, Korean masters opened their own schools (kwans) and began building a distinctly Korean art.

Timeline

  1. 1910 to 1945
    Japanese occupation of Korea. Korean martial arts are suppressed.
  2. 1955
    The name "Taekwondo" is adopted. General Choi Hong Hi, a South Korean army general, is widely called the "Father of Taekwondo".
  3. 1966
    General Choi founds the International Taekwon-Do Federation (ITF).
  4. 1972
    The Kukkiwon opens in Seoul. It is the World Taekwondo Headquarters and issues official Dan certificates.
  5. 1973
    The World Taekwondo Federation (WTF) is founded, with Kim Un-yong as its first president. It is now called World Taekwondo (WT).
  6. 1988
    Taekwondo is a demonstration sport at the Seoul Olympics.
  7. 1992
    It is a demonstration sport again, at the Barcelona Olympics.
  8. 2000
    Taekwondo becomes an official Olympic medal sport at the Sydney Olympics. Australia's Lauren Burns wins gold.

ITF and World Taekwondo

There are two main branches of Taekwondo today.

ITFFounded by General Choi. Known for its traditional patterns.
World TaekwondoLinked to the Kukkiwon. Runs Olympic Taekwondo and uses the Taeguk and Kukkiwon black belt patterns. These are the patterns AMA's black belt curriculum uses.

About AMA

Australian Martial Arts was founded in 1982 by Grand Master Ridvan. It is one of Australia's largest and longest-running martial arts schools, with more than 40 years of teaching.

Our philosophy

At AMA, martial arts is about building stronger, more resilient people, not just about fighting. We want every student to face life's challenges with confidence, integrity and perseverance. Our core values are:

  • Respect for tradition: honouring the martial arts teachings passed down through generations.
  • Community and family: students support and learn from each other, so every school feels like a family.
  • Excellence: pushing your limits, whether that's earning higher ranks, competing, or reaching personal goals.

Our main styles

AMA runs five main martial arts styles: Taekwondo, Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu, Kickboxing, Kung Fu and Tricking. Taekwondo classes start at Mini Dragons (18 months to 3 years) and go through to Adults, with Family classes as well.

The AMA belt journey (Taekwondo)

Black belt curriculum facts

  • Board breaking gets harder at each Dan: 2 directions for 2nd Dan, 3 directions for 3rd Dan, and 4 directions for 4th Dan.
  • Patterns build on each other at every level. Going for 4th Dan you perform Pal Jang, Koryo, Keumgang and Tae Baek continuously. Pyongwon is added at 4th Dan, and Sipjin is added at 4th Dan Level 2 → Level 3.
  • Kickboxing combos in black belt gradings are all done on focus mitts.
